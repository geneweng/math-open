TWO-EDGE CUTS: ENDPOINT INFORMATION AND AN EXACT INFINITE FAMILY
29 October 2026

The original conjecture remains unresolved. This continuation proves a
limitation of the three-number edge-profile strategy for general gluing,
and determines the exact optimum of an infinite family. All graphs in
that family satisfy the original bound with surplus.

1. The exact boundary law

Let H be a graph with two degree-two ports u,v and all other vertices
of degree three. For i,j in {0,1}, let C_ij be the maximum size of an
induced 2-regular set in H that selects u exactly when i=1 and v exactly
when j=1. Let P be the maximum size of a selected set containing both
ports, with selected degree one at the ports and degree two elsewhere.
Use -infinity for an impossible state. The latter state consists of one
u--v path together with disjoint cycles.

These are the only five boundary states possible in a good set after
joining ports. A selected port has internal selected degree either two
(its joining edge is unused) or one (its joining edge is used). The
number of degree-one vertices in a finite graph is even when every
other nonzero degree is two, ruling out exactly one open port.

For two pieces joined by u_1u_2 and v_1v_2, the exact optimum is
  max(P_1+P_2,
      max { C^1_ij + C^2_kl : i*k=0 and j*l=0 }).
The first term uses both joining edges. The other terms use neither:
two selected closed ports cannot be adjacent across a joining edge.
Every compatible pair of local maximizing sets gives a global good
set, and restricting any global good set gives one of these cases.
Thus this is an equality, not merely a bound. For crossed joining,
interchange the two ports of one piece.

This five-state representation was already present in the exploratory
code. The point here is to expose the mathematical limitation of replacing
it with the more recent three-number profile.

2. The information discarded by U,T,W

If H=G-uv for a cubic graph G, then
  U=max(C_00,C_01,C_10,C_11), T=P, W=C_00,
  c_ind(G)=max(C_00,C_01,C_10,P).
The asymmetric values C_01 and C_10 cannot in general be recovered.

Take the retained 18-vertex parent with graph6 string
  Q??CA?_C?WX?p?J?EA?`_?w?BG?
and delete edge (2,13). With first port u=2 and second port v=13,
its exact cardinality table is
  (C_00,C_01,C_10,C_11,P)=(9,9,11,11,8).
Consequently U=11, T=8, W=9 and the cubic parent's optimum is 11.
Reversing the ports preserves all four of those aggregate quantities.

Nevertheless, two identical pieces joined first-to-second and
second-to-first have optimum 22, whereas joining first-to-first and
second-to-second gives optimum 20. Both resulting graphs have 36 vertices.
Thus even U,T,W together with the original parent's optimum fail to
determine the optimum of a two-edge join. This example concerns general
two-edge cuts; it does not refute the earlier formulas for the two specific
rooted constructions, where U,T,W are sufficient.

3. Exact ring theorem and unbounded discrepancy

Arrange k>=2 copies of H in a ring, joining the right port of each piece
to the left port of the next. Each piece may be oriented either way.
Call port u the preferred port, because selecting it raises the closed
state value from 9 to 11, regardless of the status of v. Let t be the
number of ring edges joining two preferred ports. Then
  c_ind(G)=11k-2t.

Proof. If one joining edge is used by a good set, both ports in each
incident piece are open. This propagates around the ring. The all-open
channel has exact value 8k.

Otherwise all pieces use closed states. Their total value is 9k plus
two for each selected preferred port. Preferred-port/preferred-port
joins form a matching on the preferred ports, since each port belongs
to only one joining edge. At most k-t preferred ports can be selected.
This upper bound is achievable: select one preferred port from each of
the t pairs and all unpaired preferred ports, and leave every nonpreferred
port unselected. These states are compatible and all have local witnesses.
The closed optimum is therefore 9k+2(k-t)=11k-2t. Since t<=floor(k/2),
it is at least 10k and exceeds the all-open value 8k. QED.

Uniform orientation has t=0 and optimum 11k. For even k, alternating
orientation has t=k/2 and optimum 10k. The two families have identical
pieces, the same number of vertices, and identical local U,T,W and
parent-optimum data, yet their optima differ by k, without bound.

This is not a counterexample to the original conjecture: their common
order is 18k, so even the lower value 10k exceeds the required 9k.
It is a rigorous exclusion of this entire family and a counterexample
to treating the aggregate data as sufficient for general composition.

4. Verification and limitations

The local table is checked by exhaustive enumeration of all 2^18 vertex
subsets. Both 36-vertex examples are independently checked by enumeration
of all 524,288 cycle-space vectors each. Positive maximum witnesses are
retained. A separate five-state dynamic program checks the ring formula
for every orientation at lengths 2 through 10 (2,044 patterns). The proof
above, rather than those finite checks, establishes the infinite family.

A bounded check of the previously retained near-sharp profiles through
order 16 found no orientation-sensitive pair among those profiles. That
negative result is not extrapolated; the explicit orientation-sensitive
example above has a parent with surplus two, outside that near-sharp pool.

No general inequality governing all five realizable states has been
proved. Nor has the remaining rooted target U=h+1,T<=h-1,W<=h-2 been
excluded. The result does not reduce arbitrary cubic graphs to this
ring family and does not complete the requested proof or counterexample.
