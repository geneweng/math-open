CONTINUATION: COVERS, HALF-SET CERTIFICATES, AND THE DESCENT OBSTRUCTION
29 October 2026

The original conjecture remains unresolved. None of the statements below
proves c_ind(G)>=|G|/2 for arbitrary cubic G, or constructs a counterexample.

1. Purpose and bounded experiment

The extremal audit found that the retained equality graphs were built
from Franklin cores. We tested whether two-sheeted covers of the Franklin
graph F could supply a different equality core.

Fix a spanning tree of F. Switching sheet labels at vertices makes all
tree-edge signs positive. The seven cotree edges then have independent
binary signs. The zero assignment gives two disjoint copies; each of the
127 nonzero assignments gives a connected cover. This exhausts signed
two-sheeted covers over this fixed labelled base, not all cubic graphs.

Exact optima on these 24-vertex covers:
  c_ind=12:   6 assignments
  c_ind=14:  48 assignments
  c_ind=16:  32 assignments
  c_ind=18:  41 assignments
All six sharp covers are isomorphic to the already retained 24-vertex
graph; each has edge connectivity two. No new equality type was found.
The normalization/completeness argument does not imply the 127 covers
are pairwise nonisomorphic.

Verification: the initial optimization used CP-SAT. An independent
cycle-space enumerator checked all 8,192 vectors for each cover and
agreed on every optimum. verify.py checks cover reconstruction, witnesses,
the comparison of exact results, and the six isomorphisms.

2. Pullback theorem

Let p:H->G be a finite d-sheeted graph cover: at each vertex of H the
map on neighbours is a bijection onto the neighbours of its image.
If S is good in G, then p^{-1}(S) is good in H and has size d|S|.
Indeed, each selected lift has exactly as many selected neighbours as
its image, namely two. Uniform fibre size gives the cardinality.

Consequently every finite cover of F has complementary half-sized good
sets. More strongly, every edge of that cover belongs to a half-sized
good set: use the saved half-set certificate for its image edge in F,
and pull it back. This uses the 18 positive edge witnesses of the
extremal audit, not a conjecture about equality graphs in general.

The certificate theorem in extremal_audit/REVIEW.txt therefore applies
to every connected finite cover of F. Neither of the two rooted
constructions, at any edge of any such cover, yields a counterexample.
This explains why further searches confined to this family cannot
produce a counterexample by those operations.

3. Universal common-cover construction: a limitation of the strategy

Theorem. Every connected finite simple cubic graph G has a connected
finite cover C, of degree at most 12 over G, which also covers F.
In particular C has a complementary good partition and every edge of C
belongs to a half-sized good set.

Proof. Form the canonical bipartite double cover B of G: its vertices
are (v,b), b in {0,1}, and every edge uv gives edges (u,0)(v,1) and
(u,1)(v,0). B is cubic and bipartite, even when G has bridges.

Any finite regular bipartite graph decomposes into perfect matchings.
For completeness, if it is k-regular, counting the k|X| edges from a
subset X of one part gives |N(X)|>=|X|. Hall's theorem supplies a perfect
matching. Remove it and repeat. Thus colour the edges of B, and separately
those of the bipartite cubic F, with three colours so that every vertex
has one incident edge of each colour.

Let beta:F->{0,1} be a bipartition. Construct K with vertices
  ((v,b),f) where beta(f)=b.
Join two such vertices when their B coordinates are joined by an edge
of colour i and their F coordinates are joined by an edge of the same
colour i. At each vertex, there is exactly one neighbour of each colour.
Both coordinate projections are locally bijective; composing the first
with B->G is also locally bijective. K is a simple cubic graph.

Each vertex of G has 12 inverse images in K (six for each value of b),
so |K|=12|G|. Select any connected component C. The image of a nonempty
component under a local bijection is closed under taking neighbours.
As G and F are connected, both restricted projections are surjective.
Lifting an edge gives a bijection between the two endpoint fibres, so
the fibre size is constant over each connected base. Thus C covers both
G and F and its degree over G is at most 12. Apply the pullback theorem
to the certificates on F. QED.

This is an explicit regular-graph special case of the classical
common-cover phenomenon, not a claim of a new general covering theorem.
Leighton's graph covering theorem gives the broader result for graphs
with a common universal covering tree. Sources:
  F. T. Leighton, Finite common coverings of graphs (1982):
  https://doi.org/10.1016/0095-8956(82)90042-9
  N. Touikan and A. Minasyan, On the proofs of Leighton's Graph Covering
  Theorem, a notion dual to commensurability, and normal virtual retracts:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01839

The point for this project is negative: existence of a finite cover with
our strong certificate property places NO restriction on a cubic base.
Any approach using it must prove an additional descent/rounding theorem.
Merely generating more good covers cannot settle the original conjecture.

4. Why projecting a good set is not a valid descent argument

The cube Q3 covers K4 by identifying antipodal binary triples. A square
face of Q3 is a good set containing half its vertices. Its four vertices
lie in four different antipodal classes, so its image is ALL of K4.
That image has induced degree three, not two. Therefore a good half-set
in a cover need not project to a good set in the base, even injectively
on that selected set. Identifications elsewhere introduce extra induced
adjacencies. This does not refute the original conjecture: K4 has good
triangles. It refutes the proposed projection operation only.

The executable check constructs covers for all 112 connected cubic
graphs through order 12 and for the retained bridged graph of order 26.
It verifies local bijections in all 113 cases, uniform fibres in the
145 resulting components, and 2,900 pulled-back half-sets. It also checks
the cube-to-K4 projection example explicitly. These tests support the
implementation; the proof above is the general argument.

5. Where the original proof still fails

No general descent theorem has been established. No proof of the proposed
joint U,T,W tradeoff has been obtained. In particular, the remaining
minimum triangle-form obstruction U=h+1, T<=h-1, W<=h-2 has not been
excluded. Excluding it alone would still leave the subdivision normal
form and the general bridgeless case.

Future proof work should address a base-graph exchange inequality or a
precise rounding lemma with proof. Further cover enumeration is stopped:
the universal-cover construction explains why that direction supplies
certificates upstairs without resolving the obstruction downstairs.
