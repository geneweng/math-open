FINITE PROFILE CLOSURE FOR ARBITRARILY LONG RINGS
29 October 2026

Status: the original conjecture remains unresolved. This note proves an
infinite assembly theorem by an independently checked finite induction.
It also constructs a counterexample to a stronger vertex-deletion claim.
Neither result is a counterexample to the original conjecture.

1. Normalized five-state profiles and series composition

For a two-port piece H of even order 2h, normalize each of its five
boundary capacities by subtracting h. Write its profile as
  (a,b,c,d,p)=(C00-h,C01-h,C10-h,C11-h,P-h).
The meanings of the boundary states are as in two_cut_audit/RESULTS.txt.
Impossible states have value -infinity. All finite entries are integers.

Joining the right port of H to the left port of another piece K by one
edge leaves a two-port chain. If A and B are their 2-by-2 closed-state
matrices, the exact new closed entry is
  (A*B)[i,l] = max(A[i,0]+B[0,l],
                   A[i,0]+B[1,l],
                   A[i,1]+B[0,l]).
The new open value is p_H+p_K. In a closed state the joining edge is
unused, so its two ends cannot both be selected. In an open state the
path between the remaining ports must cross the joining edge, so both
pieces use their open states. Parity rules out any other state.
These statements prove the composition identity. It is associative and
monotone in every entry, with the usual -infinity arithmetic.

Closing a chain by joining its two remaining ports has normalized optimum
  tau(a,b,c,d,p)=max(a,b,c,p).
The closed state 11 is forbidden because the added edge would give each
selected port degree three. The open state becomes a cycle. We require
the resulting graph to be simple; in the constructions below the two
ports are nonadjacent before closure.

2. The ten-profile theorem

Let B be the following ten vectors, with '-' denoting -infinity:
       a   b   c   d   p
 A    -2   0   0   -   2
 B    -2   1   1   -   1
 C    -1   -   -   1   1
 D    -1   -   1   -   1
 E    -1   0   0   1   -
 F    -1   1   -   -   1
 G     0   -   -   -   0
 H     0   -   1   -   -
 I     0   1   -   -   -
 J     1   -   -   -   -

Say a piece satisfies the profile condition if its normalized profile
is entrywise at least one vector in B.

Theorem. Every simple cubic ring assembly of pieces satisfying the
profile condition has an induced 2-regular set containing at least
half its vertices, for arbitrary ring length, order of pieces, and
orientation. Finite series composition preserves the profile condition.

Proof. Every vector in B has tau>=0. For each of the 100 ordered pairs
(X,Y) in B, at least one Z in B satisfies Z<=X*Y entrywise. The complete
finite table of choices is in closure_table.txt; each inequality is
checked by direct integer arithmetic in verify.py, independently of
the discovery search. Reversing the ports permutes B.

If actual profiles dominate X and Y, monotonicity shows that their
composition dominates X*Y and hence Z. Induction therefore supplies a
dominated basis vector for every finite chain. Monotonicity of tau and
tau(Z)>=0 prove the half bound when the chain is closed. QED.

This is a finite-certificate proof of an infinite statement. It is not
an extrapolation from short words. Discovery terminated with complete
closure; the proof relies on the independently verified table, not on
the discovery program's termination flag.

The same condition protects both earlier rooted constructions. Put
U=max(a,b,c,d) in normalized units. Direct verification on the ten
vectors gives
  max(2U,U+p+1)>=1,
  max(2 max(U,p+2), a+3+max(U,p+2))>=3.
Both expressions are monotone. They are exactly the required bounds
for the doubled subdivision and doubled root-triangle constructions.
Thus every piece satisfying the condition also passes those two tests.

3. Realizable generators covered by the theorem

The 27 retained two-port pieces in two_pole_profiles.txt, in either
orientation, satisfy the condition. For each finite entry of their
claimed lower-bound tables, the independent verifier checks an explicit
selected vertex set. Impossible entries are used only as vacuous lower
bounds. No completeness claim about a graph census is needed here.

We additionally include the following infinite families and one
disconnected piece. Their exact profiles follow from the arguments below:

  - Delete a core edge from a completed theta graph:
      branch--branch edge:       (-1,-inf,-inf,1,1);
      branch--internal edge:     (-1,-inf,1,-inf,1), or its reversal;
      internal--internal edge:   (1,-inf,-inf,-inf,1).
  - Delete a core edge from a completed cycle:
                                (0,-inf,-inf,-inf,0).
  - Take two disjoint P13 caps with their roots as ports:
                                (-1,0,0,1,-inf).

Here completion means attaching a P13 cap by a bridge to every missing
degree incidence, as in the existing bridge-completion theorem. In this
note all missing incidences are at degree-two core vertices, one cap
per such vertex. P13 has root-in optimum 7 and root-out optimum 6;
these two values are independently checked over all 8,192 vertex subsets.

A simple theta core has two branch vertices u,v and three internally
disjoint u--v paths, with at most one path of length one. If it has D
internal vertices, its completed graph has
  n=14D+2, h=7D+1, c_ind=7D+2=h+1.
For any selected core set, selected internal vertices contribute one
vertex but force the attached cap from 7 to 6. Those contributions cancel.
Thus the score is exactly 7D plus the number of selected branch vertices.
Every cycle uses both branch vertices, and an induced cycle exists.

After deleting a core edge, a closed selected internal port is impossible:
it has only one remaining core neighbour, and its cap bridge cannot lie
on a cycle. Deleting a branch--branch edge leaves the cycle formed by
the other two paths, so only states 00 and 11 are feasible. Deleting a
branch--internal edge leaves the cycle on the other two arms, giving
states 00 and the state selecting just the branch port. Deleting an
internal--internal edge leaves that cycle avoiding both ports, so only
closed state 00 is feasible. In each case an induced path through both
branch vertices connects the ports, giving open normalized value 1.
These facts establish all three displayed theta profiles for every path
length, not just the finite examples checked by the verifier.

A cycle core of length l has l attachments, completed order 14l and
optimum 7l. Deleting a core edge leaves a path. Its only closed core set
is empty, and its open core set is the full path. The cap cancellation
gives normalized values 0 in states 00 and 22 and no other states.

For two disjoint P13 caps, the four closed sizes are 12,13,13,14 on
26 vertices; an open path cannot connect different components. This
gives the stated fifth generator.

There are 47 oriented generator entries in the retained certificate
(several different pieces can have the same numerical profile). Each
dominates a basis vector. Therefore arbitrary mixtures of these pieces,
including arbitrary theta and cycle lengths, satisfy the ring theorem.
The result concerns these specified assemblies, not arbitrary cubic
graphs or arbitrary networks of higher-port pieces.

4. A stronger vertex-deletion statement is false

One tempting shortcut would assert c_ind(G-v)>=|G|/2 for every cubic G
and every vertex v. The completed theta family disproves it.

Deleting a branch vertex u leaves an acyclic core. No core vertex can
then lie in a selected cycle, and all cycles lie in the attached caps.
Each cap contributes at most 7, and all can attain 7 simultaneously.
Hence
  c_ind(G-u)=7D=h-1,
whereas c_ind(G)=h+1. The smallest example in this stated family uses
theta path lengths (1,2,2), i.e. the diamond core. It has 30 vertices,
cind(G)=16, and cind(G-u)=14<15. No claim of global minimality is made.
Its edges and witnesses are saved in verification.json. Every good set
of size at least half includes both branch vertices.

This explains why the easy closed-state condition observed in the old
library cannot be assumed for all pieces. The full five-state induction
handles these additional profiles without making that false assumption.

5. Remaining gap and reproducibility

Subsequent work in profile_scope/RESULTS.txt gives an explicit realizable
profile outside these ten vectors, so that universal claim is FALSE.
The conditional theorem proved here remains valid. Nor is every cubic
graph known to decompose into the
pieces covered here. Those missing statements cannot be inferred from
the successful closure check. The original conjecture and the remaining
rooted bad-profile target are not resolved.

Reproduce from the repository root:
  python3 cubic_conjecture_search/ring_closure/verify.py

The verifier uses no optimization solver. It checks 148 positive source
witnesses, the generator-to-basis relation, all 100 ordered basis products,
the cap table, small representative theta core profiles, and the displayed
30-vertex witnesses. The general theta formulas and induction above supply
the infinite arguments. discover.py regenerates the search certificate;
verify.py checks it independently using exact -infinity arithmetic.
The main paper and PDF are unchanged.
